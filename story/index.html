Our story

Two kitchens, one fire

Ember & Saffron began as an argument between a mother in Shiraz and a grandmother in Lahore about the right way to make rice. We never settled it. We built a restaurant around it instead.

I · Origins

A boy between two stoves

Darius Mehrani grew up in Ealing with a Persian mother and a Pakistani grandmother who shared one small kitchen and very few opinions. One cooked rice to be fluffy and separate; the other wanted it to cling. Both were right.

He trained at Le Gavroche and spent three winters at a wood-fired restaurant outside Copenhagen, where he learned that fire is not a cooking method but an ingredient.

II · The hearth

Everything passes through the fire

Our kitchen is built around a single open hearth fed with seasoned oak and fruitwood from a woodland in Kent. Some dishes sit in its embers for four hours; some pass through its flames for four seconds.

Nothing is wasted. Ash seasons the lamb, embers smoke the yoghurt, and the last of the fire warms the tea at the end of the night.

III · Sourcing

Small farms, long friendships

Saffron comes from a women's cooperative in Torbat-e Heydarieh, bought directly at harvest. Lamb is from a single flock on the Romney Marsh. Our rice is aged basmati from a family mill in Punjab that has supplied Darius's family for three generations.

We print our suppliers on every menu. If you would like to visit them, ask — most of them would be delighted.
