Our philosophy

We cook the food our grandmothers argued about, over the fire they would have trusted.

Every dish passes through the hearth: some for four hours, some for four seconds. Nothing is rushed, and nothing is wasted. Even the ash becomes seasoning.

The tasting menu

The Seven Courses

One menu, served to every table, written in seven chapters from arrival to the last ember.

£145 per guest · wine pairing £95 · non-alcoholic pairing £65

  1. IArrival

    Anar

    Pomegranate · Sumac · Labneh · Walnut

    The evening opens the way a Tehran courtyard does in autumn: a split pomegranate, cold labneh, and the sour hush of sumac.

  2. IIThe spice box

    Masala Dabba

    Green cardamom · Fennel · Cassia · Lentil broth

    A clear lentil broth poured at the table over seven toasted spices, each one from my grandmother's tin box in Lahore.

  3. IIIThe river

    Mahi

    Saffron-cured trout · Kohlrabi · Dill oil · Lime

    Trout cured for nine hours in saffron and salt, finished at the pass with dill oil and the brightest lime we can find.

  4. IVThe hearth

    Atash

    Lamb neck · Barberry · Charcoal ash · Smoked yoghurt

    The heart of the evening. Lamb neck turned over oak embers for four hours, glazed with barberry and dusted with its own ash.

  5. VThe garden

    Anjir

    Black fig · Duck liver · Rose · Brioche

    Roast black figs and a whisper of rose, set on warm brioche the way a Mughal garden sets a fountain: off-centre, on purpose.

  6. VIThe table

    Tahdig

    Saffron rice · Butter · Quince · Slow-cooked chicken

    Every Persian family fights over the crust. Ours is turned out whole at your table, golden and crackling, with quince khoresh.

  7. VIIThe last ember

    Shirin

    Saffron ice cream · Pistachio · Pomegranate · Brass-tray tea

    Saffron bastani with pistachio and the last of the pomegranates, then cardamom tea poured from brass as the fire burns down.

The kitchen

Darius MehraniChef–patron

“My mother cooked in Shiraz, my father's mother in Lahore. I grew up between their two kitchens and never chose a side. This restaurant is me refusing to.”

Darius trained in London and Copenhagen before opening Ember & Saffron in 2024 with a single charcoal hearth and a team of nine. Every menu begins in his notebook and ends at the fire.
